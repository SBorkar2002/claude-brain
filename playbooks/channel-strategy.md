# Channel strategy playbook (AI FOMO / employer-brand YouTube)

The thinking behind the YouTube channel Sejal built at Epifi (Aug to Oct 2026), how it was argued, what was decided, and what stayed open. Reusable for any future channel she pitches or runs: the methods (subtractive test, lens, topic filter) transfer even though this channel ended with the company (wound down Nov 2026).

Script craft is in `script-writing.md`; mistakes are in `../lessons/script-traps.md`; raw history in `../memory/project-youtube-employer-brand.md` and `../memory/project-shorts-experiment.md`.

---

## 1. What exists
- **Channel:** AI FOMO (handle AI FOMO Lab). Name came from the CEO in a review: "we all have AI FOMO". Banner idea: two devs at opposite corners (one sipping coffee), channel name between them, no logo. Avatar from the channel bumper still.
- **Series:** *I Have No Idea What That Means* (Anandhi suggested the series name from the signature line). Comic shorts, one AI/eng term per episode, 9:16, 1 to 3 minutes. Cuts rendered: `loop` (EP001), `graph engineering`, `remote control`, `/insights`, `system one model`, `prompt engineering` (v1 render 20 Oct); scripts also for `subagent`, `context window`, `skill`, a channel bumper, and a 7 to 15s shorts experiment (`make it better`).
- **Posted to:** YouTube Shorts, Instagram Reels, TikTok (TikTok reach was unusable; not something to write down).
- **What performed:** the AJ intern episode (`graph engineering`) was the breakout on Instagram, roughly 20x the others. Character-led comedy with a clear want and a loop did best. Saves and shares matter more than views as the signal.

## 2. Objective and audience
- **Objective:** employer-brand / practitioner reach, **zero product content**. Not a buyer pipeline (that is the launch videos). Terms, problem statements, struggles, how to overcome them. Fun, spreadable.
- **CEO's bar (21 Aug):** YouTube is a marketing play, not a brand build. Below creator scale it isn't worth doing; frequency is what makes a creator. Target cadence 2 to 3 videos a week, a 90-day consistency gate, ~1.5 to 2 min videos (he preferred these over very short Shorts).
- **Audience (Anandhi's sharpest insight):** the engineer who is NOT an AI power user and feels the ground moving. The 9-to-5 engineer watching "I built 5 agents this weekend" feeds, quietly scared of being left behind. Ladder starts at "your first skill / first agent / the dumbest way to approach a big concept", not at loop engineering.
- **The emotional engine:** asking costs status, and costs more the more senior you are. So everyone nods and nobody asks. Everyone is a tourist here, staff engineer and new grad alike, so no audience narrowing is needed.
- **Rejected audience framings:** "AI for people with actual jobs" (Sejal caught that it excludes students and laid-off engineers, the most anxious and motivated segment) and its rephrase "AI for people who use it, not people who post about it".

## 3. How the spine was found (the method is the reusable part)
Timeline of the argument, so a future Claude can rerun it:
1. **18 Aug, direction:** multiple buckets (fun / educational / influencer-led / pure visual) that must connect; formats can vary but videos can't be unrelated one-offs. Research the whole landscape and adjacent spaces (fintech socials, Morning Brew, Duolingo, museum accounts), extract larger themes, then form a point of view. Don't ask "which brand does exactly this": nobody does. Every decision needs a stated "why"; the strategy IS the reasoning chain.
2. **19 Aug, spine v1, "The Phrasebook":** the channel as the visible surface of a plain-English reference written in public; every video is one entry read aloud. Filter: "does it produce or clarify an entry?" Shorts = one entry, long = a cluster, playlists = chapters in dependency order. Sejal rejected tagline-shaped answers twice: she wanted a **shape**, not a line. A central theme can be an artifact, a territory, a behaviour, or (later) a feeling.
3. **20 Aug, phrasebook demoted to a device.** Anandhi: "a credit scene does not make the movie." Tools from that review:
   - **Subtractive test:** take the idea out; does anything you make change? If not, it's a device, not a lens.
   - **Rejection probe:** ask "why can't I do AI ASMR?" A working filter must be able to reject it. A filter about output format can't; it has to be about who is speaking and why.
   - **A lens must answer four things:** who speaks (face or not), their attitude (authority / peer / learner), why come back tomorrow (a challenge, a cliffhanger), how it stays topical (tie to what is happening in AI now).
   - **Casper shape / jingle test:** name + descriptor that pre-decides behaviour ("Casper the friendly ghost", "your everyday AI coach").
   - Three lenses compared: The Authority, The Learner, The Everyday Coach. Recommendation given: **the Learner, but senior** ("I've shipped for twelve years and I still didn't know what an MCP server was"). Do not blend lenses; a lens works because it forbids.
4. **Kurzgesagt study:** their lens is a **feeling** (optimistic nihilism), not a person. The transferable stance for this audience: "this is smaller than it looks, and you are not behind." Calm against hype. Steal the constraint discipline (one tight visual language held absolutely), not the production budget. Note: Kurzgesagt is evergreen and low-frequency, which is counter-evidence to "must be topical, must be daily".
5. **21 Aug, CEO spec (what actually got built):** meme cold open (engineer hears a term he doesn't know) → plain, correct breakdown (1 to 1.5 min) → quick get-started tip → **punchline that completes the meme**. Comedy as a bracket around straight teaching. Sejal's comic answer to this spec won: nobody is doing comics in the AI space, and a reusable cast makes each new episode cheap (the cadence argument).
6. **The phrasebook survived as a component** (a mid-film "entry card" with term and definition), then end cards were dropped entirely on 5 Oct.

**Effective spine of what shipped:** *everyone is nodding, nobody is asking; the AI is the safe place to ask.* The signature thought caption carries it.

## 4. Formats on the table
Sejal's own evaluation questions for any format idea (use them): who is the audience; does it work for someone who isn't Gen Z; is it scalable; can it be reused in other videos; how do we keep making content with it; will it be controversial; what do we avoid. Plus: how it hooks, how it fails, how it builds the brand.

| Format | Status | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Comic series (IHNIWTM)** | Shipped | Term inside a joke, cast reused, greyscale + one teal accent, hard cuts. |
| **Two Voices** (one dumb asker, one smart answerer) | Ranked first-to-ship in the 19 Aug deck, not built | Anandhi couldn't picture it from a description: bring a reference video for any format pitch. |
| **The Ladder** (term for a 10-year-old, a grad student, a staff engineer) | Ranked first-to-ship, not built | Needs a named staff engineer for rung 3. Lives on as the comic's "dense rung → blank face → plain rung". |
| **Question Compilation** (real Reddit/Quora "too scared to ask" questions) | Next | **Never fabricate screenshots**; anonymise real ones or collect via a form. |
| **Wrong Answer First** (myth, then debunk) | Held | Finite supply (~30 myths). |
| Pop quiz | Dropped by Sejal | |
| Illustrated host / V-tuber / mascot | Deferred | Sejal: nail the spine first; a character never derived from the spine, so it can be layered later. |
| Channel bumper (~12s) | Scripted | The only fourth-wall break allowed: Dev 1 glances at camera, "...you too?", "all the stuff people say at your desk that you just nod at. actually explained." |
| **7 to 15s looping shorts** | Experiment from 14 Oct | No term, no explainer, no end card; proven character + reused plates so only one variable changes; works with sound off; hook is the rhythm (several hard cuts in the first 4s); holds 0.3 to 0.5s. |

What the tools can make (one person, Remotion + generated stills): typography, diagram builds, simulated UI/terminal/diff, data, geometric motion are cheap and look deliberate. AI-generated video as the spine and generated illustration as the main look read as "AI sloppy", which this audience spots instantly.

## 5. Topic filter (a term must pass all five; four of five is a rejection)
1. **Someone would say it out loud** at standup, in a PR, in Slack. A noun, not a trend or headline. (`loop`, `subagent`, `context window`, `eval` yes; "AI code quality is measurable now" no.)
2. **A senior person would be embarrassed not to know it.** Would a lead nod rather than ask? A word brand new to everyone fails (nobody loses face); a word so basic it's disqualifying fails too.
3. **You can show the mechanism, not just define it.** "What's on the screen?" must have an answer (log counting down, chat scrolled impossibly far, prompt box overflowing).
4. **The term's own absurdity suggests an ending** (structural, accumulation, self-demonstration, callback, inversion). No candidate ending = park it, not kill it.
5. **It isn't product.** Drop anything that mentions the company's product, a competitor, a feature or a buying decision, however strong the signal.

Extra lessons: a vendor-coined term only weeks old (`system one model`) failed test 2 and drifted toward test 5 (the episode's how-to copied the vendor's console); if forced, cut the vendor flow to a panel or two. Topicality matters (Anandhi asked twice that content tie to what is happening in AI now), but evergreen is a valid model too.

**Evidence, put simply:** for a blog, strong evidence means the trend is real; for this series, it means **people don't know this word and won't admit it** (definitional searches, apologetic Reddit questions).

**Topic pipeline:** content teammate runs theme discovery (a `/content-engine` tool), filters themes into terms, and a Monday digest of ~5 topical options; know the next 4 to 10 terms ahead without building them; engineers fact-check in one batched pass.

## 6. Commercial bridge (if the channel ever needs to connect to a product)
**One rule: the channel never mentions the product; the product may cite the channel.** Bridges in order: authorship byline (day 1) → newsletter/sales use → content only the company can make from its aggregate data (month 3+) → the org-level question a reference can't answer for one person (month 6+). Precedent: a free industry report that organised a whole product category. What breaks it: bending the topic list to match the roadmap. A distribution plan proposing "one product mention per ten posts" contradicted the zero-product brief and had to be raised.

## 7. Titles, packaging, distribution
- **Cold-start titles:** relatable, lowercase, mildly pathetic, term included for search: "my coworker said 'loop' and i just nodded". Kurzgesagt cadence (reckless question or impossible-sounding declarative, second person) works if the term stays in: "What Happens When You Leave a Loop Running?" Pure-signature titles ("riiiight. a ___.") only once there's an audience that recognises them. Provocative beats listicle ("4 mistakes you're already making" > "10 things you should know").
- **No branded intros** (they kill retention); any recurring furniture must BE the hook, costing zero seconds.
- **Captions burned in**; most people watch muted. A 9:16 master cross-posted unchanged to Shorts, Reels and TikTok. Shorts must be under 3:00.
- **Don't post as a brand:** a human account plus YouTube's collaborator feature ("how do you not behave like a brand despite being a brand"). Shorts are the entryway; brand only in long form.
- Always a GitHub link; keep on-camera mistakes/retries in (for any human-led format); build playlists deliberately, in dependency order.
- A "save this for later" pill was the only CTA tried on screen.
- Off-YouTube extensions requested by the manager: newsletter, LinkedIn, sales enablement, a future product launch video. A 9-post-per-episode cascade was proposed by the distribution owner but had no owner or cost estimate.

## 8. Operating rules that kept it alive
- **Cadence is the gate, not quality.** Fixed cast and reused plates are what make 2 to 3 a week survivable. Mark plate reuse in scripts.
- **Rough voice pass before any art** (cheapest kill gate). Static storyboard first, have two or three people read it cold; animate only if they get it.
- **Don't iterate further on the format until feedback on the pilot comes back** (explicit instruction early on).
- Don't depend on an engineer per video; batch fact-checks.
- Ownership split that worked: ideation + distribution with the content teammate; script + visual production with Sejal. Loop in a second person early so Sejal isn't a single point of failure.
- Script lives in three places on purpose: review artifact (comments), repo markdown (frozen record), shared drive export (org archive). Account loss is a real risk; it happened.

## 9. Open questions (never resolved)
- Is the left-behind engineer the locked audience; real face vs synthetic persona vs none.
- Brand channel vs a separate neutral channel; would the reference/phrasebook sit on a company or neutral domain, and its name.
- Shorts-to-long ratio and the real cadence (30 videos a month was questioned; any number before one finished episode's hours and credits were known was a guess).
- Whether shorts (7 to 15s) beat the 1 to 3 min episodes; whether the shorts experiment's peak frame may break the greyscale + teal palette.
- Reels-first (distribution plan) vs ~2-min YouTube (CEO preference).
- Whether the AI mark reads as too close to a specific AI vendor (raised twice by the manager).
- Which "grimy screen" maths channel the manager referenced: unclear from history.
