# Concept Art — Project 01 Brief

A self-set brief structured the way a studio portfolio piece is expected to look.
Purpose: prove you can *design*, not just render. That is the one thing your
ArtStation does not currently show.

---

## The point of this project

Your 38 pieces prove rendering. They do not prove design, because in fanart the
design problem was already solved by someone else — silhouette, costume logic,
palette, how the character reads at distance, what the world implies.

This project exists to show you solving that from nothing, **with the thinking visible**.
The final illustration is the least important deliverable here. The thumbnails and
iterations are the portfolio.

---

## The brief

**Working title: The Last Hands**

*Premise.* Fabrication is fully automated. Anything can be made, instantly, perfectly.
So the only thing left with value is the word of a human who certifies that something
was made, checked, or judged by a person. A guild grew up around this. They are not
opposed to the machines. They are the signature at the bottom of the page.

*The design problem.* Design three members of this guild at three ranks, so that a
viewer can tell rank apart **by silhouette alone, with no colour and no text.**

Why this premise: it is close enough to work you have actually done for six months
that you will have real opinions, which makes design decisions faster and makes you
articulate in an interview. If you would rather build a different world, keep the
**structure** below and swap the premise. The structure is the part that matters.

### Constraints

Constraints are what force design decisions, so do not loosen these.

1. **Rank must read in silhouette.** No colour, no insignia, no labels.
2. **One accent colour only**, against an otherwise desaturated palette. You already
   work this way (greyscale plus teal on the comic series). Use it.
3. **Every guild member carries a physical mark of what they have certified.** You
   decide the form. It must accumulate with experience, so it doubles as the rank
   system.
4. **Nothing purely decorative.** Every element on the design answers "what is this
   for in this world."
5. **No AI at any stage.** Not for ideation, not for reference generation, not for
   fixing a hand. The entire value of this piece is that it is clean, and you must be
   able to say so without qualification.

### Deliverables

- 3 character designs: Apprentice, Journeyman, Master
- 1 prop set: the tools of certification, 4 to 6 objects
- 1 environment: where certification happens
- 1 hero illustration (one character, fully rendered — this is where your strength goes)
- The full process, documented

---

## The process, in order

This is also the order you present it in.

**Phase 1 — Research and reference.** A board of real-world sources: guild systems,
craft marks, hallmarking, trade hierarchies, workwear that shows seniority through wear.
Real references, not other people's concept art. 1 to 2 days.

**Phase 2 — Silhouette thumbnails.** 30 to 50 of them, small, fast, black shapes only.
No detail, no faces. The point is to find shape language. Most will be bad; that is the
exercise. **Photograph or export the whole sheet — this goes in the portfolio.** 2 to 3 days.

**Phase 3 — Narrow and iterate.** Pick 3 silhouettes that read as a family but differ by
rank. Do 3 to 4 variations of each, changing one decision at a time. Annotate what each
variation is testing. 4 to 5 days.

**Phase 4 — Refine to final line.** Clean designs, full body, front view. Still flat or
minimal rendering. Resist finishing them beautifully at this stage. 4 to 5 days.

**Phase 5 — Callout sheet.** For each character: a turnaround (front, three-quarter, back),
colour callouts, material notes, and short annotations explaining *why*. This is the single
most studio-legible artifact you can make and almost no illustrator has one. 3 to 4 days.

**Phase 6 — Props and environment.** The tools, then one wide establishing view of the
certification hall. Environments are your weakest area, so keep the first one simple:
one interior, one light source, clear depth. 5 to 7 days.

**Phase 7 — Hero illustration.** One character, fully rendered, at the quality your
ArtStation already shows. Last, not first. 3 to 4 days.

**Total: roughly four to six weeks** at a part-time pace.

---

## How to present it

Order on the page matters as much as the work:

1. One-line premise
2. Hero illustration (the hook that makes someone keep scrolling)
3. The three final designs together, showing the rank language
4. Turnaround and callout sheets
5. Iteration sheets with annotations
6. Silhouette thumbnail sheet
7. Props, environment
8. Two or three lines on what you would change

Hero first because it earns attention. Process after because it earns the interview.

## Honest expectations

One project does not make you a concept artist. Three do. This is the first, and the
point is to prove the format to yourself before repeating it twice more with different
subject matter (a creature or vehicle set, and an environment-led piece).

Keep the product design track as the thing that pays while this builds. Six months of
deliberate work gets you a credible concept portfolio. Nothing gets you there faster,
and the rendering ability you already have is the part most people are missing.

---

## One thing to settle early

Decide how the AI work on your resume sits alongside this. Your personal art is hand-drawn;
the PocketFM and comic series roles were AI pipeline work. Both are true. An art director
will find both. Have a plain, unembarrassed sentence ready, and decide in advance how
prominent the AI roles are on any profile you send to a studio.
