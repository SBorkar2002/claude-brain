Three Lenses

Employer-brand channel  /  the lens decision

Three Lenses

The phrasebook was a device, not a lens — it survives the review as a component, but it can't be the thing everything derives from. These are three lenses that can be.

00 — WHAT A LENS HAS TO DO

Four questions, and one test

The phrasebook answered none of these, which is why it read as brand recall rather than strategy. Any candidate has to answer all four.

Who is speaking? A face, a person, an institution, or nobody — but it has to be decided, not deferred.

What is their attitude? Authority, peer, or learner. This sets tone, who we partner with, and every title we write.

Why does anyone come back tomorrow? A reference has no cliffhanger. Something else has to supply the pull.

How does it stay topical? Everything has to connect to what's happening in AI right now, not to an evergreen list.

The subtractive test

Take the lens away. If the content is unchanged, it was a device, not a lens. Remove "music for everyone" and the vernacular billboards stop making sense — that's what passing looks like.

Lens 01

The Authority

"The place engineers check before they believe the hype."

Who speaks

An institution, not a person. An editorial desk — can stay faceless, or rotate real senior engineers as guests.

Attitude

Measured, unimpressed, above the noise. Never excited.

Why return

You trust one filter more than fifty threads. The pull is reliability, not narrative.

Topicality

Built in — it is a news function. Strongest of the three on this.

Cadence

Daily short brief plus a weekly deep dive. High volume, and high input cost per unit.

Shorts / long

Shorts carry the day's one thing; long form is the weekly synthesis. Brand appears only in long form.

Phrasebook

Becomes the reference desk — the glossary a publication maintains. Natural, unremarkable fit.

Licenses
  • Daily "what actually shipped" brief
  • Deep dives, whiteboard explainers
  • PhD and senior-engineer collaborations
  • Benchmark and claim scrutiny
Forbids
  • Learning in public — an authority can't be a beginner
  • Vlogs, personal narrative
  • Challenges and streaks
  • AI ASMR — an authority does not whisper
Wrong Answer Firstnative — correction is the core job
The Ladderworks — expert breaking it down
Question Compilationtone clash with "too scared to ask"
Two Voicesundermines it — no naive foil needed
Biggest risk

We have no authority yet, and authority is the one thing that can't be bootstrapped by deciding to have it. It also needs senior engineering time daily — the resourcing hole from the v1 doc, multiplied. And it's the most crowded position in the category.

Subtractive test: passes — remove "authority" and the daily brief has no rationale and corrections have no standing.

Lens 02

The Learner

"An engineer learning this in public — including the parts that don't work."

Who speaks

One named engineer, consistently. A real person, or at minimum a consistent real voice with a real repo behind it.

Attitude

Peer, fallible, curious. Competent at their job and openly at the start of this one thing.

Why return

The arc. You're watching someone get better — and a challenge structure gives you a genuine cliffhanger, which a glossary never can.

Topicality

Automatic. A learner learns whatever just shipped, so the calendar writes itself.

Cadence

Daily short (today's step) plus a weekly long (the build, or the failure, in full). The challenge sets the schedule for you.

Shorts / long

Shorts are the entryway; long form is where the build gets explained and where the brand plugs in naturally — "here's what I used to find where I got stuck."

Phrasebook

Best fit of the three. It becomes the notebook they're filling — every term learned is a page, and the sidebar growing is the learning arc made visible.

Licenses
  • 100-day and n-day challenges
  • "What I learned today"
  • Failure logs, live attempts, retries
  • Asking real experts for help on camera
  • Building something using only skills
Forbids
  • Authoritative pronouncements
  • Tier lists, hot takes, rankings
  • Anything polished or first-try perfect
  • AI ASMR — a learning diary isn't ambient
Two Voicesnative — the learner is the asker
Question Compilationnative — peer to peer, can't condescend
Wrong Answer First"I believed this. I was wrong."
The Ladderworks — learner reaching upward
Biggest risk

It needs a real, sustained person, so the bus factor is severe — if they leave, the channel dies. And there's a brand tension: does a company selling engineering intelligence want to be publicly "figuring it out"? See the resolution note below, because this is more fixable than it looks.

Subtractive test: passes strongly — remove "the learner" and the challenges have no protagonist and the failures mean nothing.

Lens 03

The Everyday Coach

"One thing a day, for engineers who don't have time to keep up."

Who speaks

A warm, patient presence. Slightly senior but never lecturing — a coach meets you where you are rather than delivering a syllabus.

Attitude

Encouraging, practical, unhurried. Never makes the viewer feel late.

Why return

Habit, not narrative. The pull is a daily routine — closer to a morning newsletter or a streak than to a story.

Topicality

Weakest here. A coach has no obligation to react to news, so topicality needs deliberate scaffolding — the Monday digest from Prashasti is the fix.

Cadence

Literally daily. Highest volume of the three, lowest cost per unit, because a tip is cheap to make.

Shorts / long

Shorts dominate; long form is the weekly consolidation. Brand appears only in long form.

Phrasebook

Becomes the curriculum. The dependency ordering stops being tidiness and becomes pedagogy.

Licenses
  • One concept a day, today's tip
  • Topical reaction with a practical spin
  • Weekly recap and consolidation
  • Small challenges, streaks
Forbids
  • Long undirected essays
  • Hype coverage of any kind
  • Anything that implies the viewer is behind
  • AI ASMR — a coach speaks to you, not at ambient
The Laddernative — three rungs, three coachees
Question Compilationnative — coaching is answering
Wrong Answer Firstworks — gentle correction
Two Voicesworks — coach and coachee
Biggest risk

Generic. "Daily tip" channels are everywhere and this is the least differentiated of the three. It's also closest to the product, which cuts both ways — the best commercial tie, and the most likely to read as marketing.

Subtractive test: passes, weakly — remove "coach" and the daily cadence loses its rationale, but the content survives largely intact.

Note that cost and bus factor run against the Learner while everything else runs for it. That's the real trade in this decision, and it's a resourcing conversation rather than a creative one.

05 — RECOMMENDATION

The Learner, but senior

The Learner wins on differentiation, episodic pull, topicality, and employer-brand fit — and it's the only lens where the phrasebook becomes native rather than bolted on. Its two weaknesses are real: it needs a sustained person, and there's a tension in a company that sells engineering intelligence being publicly unsure.

That tension dissolves with one adjustment. The learner doesn't have to be junior. A senior engineer learning a genuinely new thing isn't incompetent — it's the most relatable position available, and it's exactly where the audience is standing.

The line that makes it work

"I've shipped for twelve years and I still didn't know what an MCP server was."

That is a far stronger employer-brand signal than claiming expertise, because it says our engineers are allowed to be wrong in public. It also sits cleanly with the existing position that coaching serves the whole distribution rather than extracting from top performers — the same claim, told from the inside.

Do not blend them

A lens works because it forbids things. Running Authority and Learner together forbids nothing, and we'd be back to a list of interesting ideas with no way to reject the next one. Pick one, run it for three months, and let the second exist as a playlist inside it at most.

06 — STILL OPEN

What has to be settled before this is a pitch

  • Who is the person, if it's the Learner. This is the whole lens, and it's a commitment from a real engineer, not a casting decision.
  • Brand channel or separate channel. Still a hunch. The Learner argues for separate with the brand cited in long form; the Authority argues for the brand channel directly.
  • The creator research. Ten AI-education creators — what they post, whether shorts carry them, and how they use long form. Early finding already in hand: on educational channels shorts frequently out-perform the long videos they point to.
  • Operating boundaries. Maximum shorts length, posting frequency, and where the brand may appear. These are the calls nobody will contest once stated, and their absence is what makes a plan feel like a hunch.
  • The name and descriptor in Casper shape — the name plus the two words that pre-decide what every episode does.
