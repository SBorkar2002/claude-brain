YouTube Format Slate

Four recurring formats to carry whatever content we're handed, plus one layer that sits on top of any of them. For the engineer who feels left behind and doesn't know where to start.

Ranked on durable impact — how hard the format is still working at video 50, not how well video 1 lands. Supply, fatigue and brand depth outweigh raw hook.

The Ladder

One term, explained three times — to a 10-year-old, a new grad, then a staff engineer.

Works
Three passes at the same term, each one deeper. Every glossary entry becomes exactly one video.
Hook
Two hooks in one video. The 10-year-old pass is the part that gets shared; the staff pass is why people who already know the term keep watching.
Scale
One-to-one with the glossary we're building anyway. Topic generation costs nothing, permanently.

02

Question Compilation

Real, anonymised questions from engineers too senior to ask — answered plainly.

Hook
Scale
Brand
Durability
Works
Three to five anonymised question cards per video, each answered without a trace of condescension.
Hook
Recognition. The viewer sees their own unasked question on screen — the highest emotional hit of the four.
Scale
Infinite, and it compounds: the longer the anonymous form runs, the better the supply gets.
Brand
The most on-thesis format we have. It says the fear out loud, then says it's fine.
WatchNever fabricate a screenshot. Anonymise fully — no username, no avatar, no pixel-accurate platform mock. This audience cross-checks for sport, and one fake ends the channel's credibility permanently.
03

Two Voices

An impatient sceptic asks; a calm voice answers. No faces — two type treatments.

Hook
Scale
Brand
Durability
Works
Two audibly distinct voices, two type treatments, nothing else required. The script is the video.
Hook
The asker says what the viewer is thinking about two seconds before they think it. That timing is the entire craft.
Scale
Cheapest here by a wide margin. Four to five a week once templated.
Brand
Says: we explain things without making you feel small.
WatchThe asker is sceptical, never slow. A strawman insults an audience that already feels small. Also confirm the two TTS voices are clearly distinguishable before building anything on this — it's a hard blocker.
04

Wrong Answer First

State an engineering myth confidently, as though it were true. Then take it apart.

Hook
Scale
Brand
Durability
Works
Ten to fifteen seconds of confident, plausible, wrong. Then the demolition.
Hook
The strongest structure of the four. Believed it? That's tension. Didn't? That's superiority. Both retain.
Scale
Supply-limited. Roughly thirty genuine myths exist in this space right now — a seam, not a well.
Brand
Positions us as the correction layer. Authority without arrogance.
WatchA myth nobody actually believes reads as condescending, and the debunk has to land clean — "well, it's complicated" kills the format. Spend this seam once there's an audience to spend it on.

The layer

not ranked

Sits on top of any of the four above — so it isn't ranked against them. It's a presentation layer, not a bucket, and every format still works if we drop it.

LYR

Illustrated Character

A static character with a small pose library, anchored in one bottom corner.

Hook
Scale
Brand
Durability
Works
Fronts any format. The top of frame carries the content; the character reacts from a reserved corner.
Hook
Mostly the thumbnail. A face is the click — in-video it's ambient, not the draw.
Scale
Front-loaded. Build six poses once, then near-zero cost per video. Cap it at six.
Brand
The strongest recall asset available to us. A character is a brand.
WatchA bottom-anchored character collides with the bottom-centre caption band. Reserve a corner in the templates now, not at render time. And if the character reads cute, it undercuts the whole anxiety wedge.

Ship order

impact rank ≠ build order
Now

Two Voices and The Ladder, in parallel. Two Voices is the cheapest thing we can ship and it teaches us the channel's voice fast. The Ladder rides the glossary we're building regardless.

Next

Question Compilation, once the anonymous form has run a few weeks and there's real supply to draw from.

Hold

Wrong Answer First. The supply is finite — spend it when there's an audience to spend it on, not on video three.

Layer on

Illustrated Character, added to whichever format is already working. Not before.

One structural risk

Question Compilation and Two Voices are the same underlying shape — someone asks, someone answers. Individually they're fine; back to back they'll make the channel feel monotonous somewhere around video 25, and it'll be hard to diagnose because nothing is obviously wrong. Never schedule them consecutively, or swap one for a structurally different device.
