The Nod

Somebody says a word. You nod. You have no idea what it means. Ninety seconds later, you do.

Runtime 1:30–2:00  ·  Cadence 2–3×/week  ·  Shape meme open → breakdown → punchline (varies — see §02)  ·  Per video one term, one get-started, links below  ·  Goal you can hold the conversation and start using it

01

Nobody asks, so we ask for them

Someone uses a word. You nod, because asking costs status — and the more senior you are, the more it costs. So you ask the machine instead, privately, and ninety seconds later you can hold the conversation.

That's the engine, and it's the same in every episode: everyone is nodding, nobody is asking. The shame is the hook; the AI is the safe place to take it.

The comedy is a bracket, not a layer. It opens and closes; the middle is straight teaching. That's what keeps it from turning cringe — the joke never sits on top of the lesson, so it can't undercut it.

Three rules that hold it together

  • Slang lives in the AI's mouth only. The devs never use it. Funnier that the machine is the online one, and it protects the older half of the audience — the human stays their proxy. Two or three beats per script, maximum.
  • The AI has no face. Ever. It is only a screen and a caption. No avatar, no eyes, no character.
  • No speech bubbles. All dialogue is captions — see §03. This changes the art, not just the post.
02

What repeats, and what must not

A runner is meant to repeat and gets funnier for it. A punchline is a surprise, and surprise does not survive repetition. Confusing the two is how a format dies around episode three.

Repeats every episodeChanges every episode
“Riiiight. A ___.” + I have no idea what that meansThe final joke. Derived from that term's own absurdity, never from a template.
The stamp — TERM · #N · CLEARED
The end card and the ask

Three engines to write endings with

  • Structural — the form repeats the content. Ep 01: the loop restarts. Only available when the term is about repetition.
  • Callback — plant a line mid-episode, pay it off in a new mouth at the end. Ep 02: “I have a guy.”
  • Inversion — turn the machine's flaw onto the humans. Candidate for Ep 03.

The signature carries recognition; the punchline carries the laugh. That split is also what gives the writing somewhere new to go each week, which matters more over ninety days than any single episode does.

03

Captions, not bubbles

Bubbles have to be drawn, placed and sized per panel, and they're the single most common thing image generation gets wrong. Captions are added in post, which means panels generate clean — far fewer retries, and full control of the type.

  • Burn them in. Most feed viewing is muted, so open captions are the ones that actually get read. YouTube's own CC track carries accessibility on top.
  • Fixed band, bottom. Same position every panel, so the eye stops hunting.
  • Attribution is visual. Human panels show a person, AI panels show the screen — the alternation does the work. Only tag a speaker when a panel has two people in it.
  • Emphasis sits above the band, never inside it.
One consequence

The storyboard below was generated with bubbles. Empty bubbles plus captions underneath reads as a mistake, so those panels need regenerating with the no-bubble instruction — or the bubbles get used and the caption band is dropped. Pick one; they can't coexist.

The punchline is panels 17–18

Panel 16 only implies the cycle repeats. Panels 17–18 make it happen — same framing, same words, new person — and cutting the AI mid-sentence is the timing. Both panels are free: 4 and 5 with a head swap.

This is the one episode where the comic doesn't joke about the term — it is the term. The form comments on the content, which is why the ending works here and nowhere else. Don't carry it forward; see §02.

What the viewer leaves with

A loop is stepwise, self-checked work with nobody in the middle. It needs a finish line. And you set the step cap and the budget — panel 11 does that job, which is why the AI is the responsible one and the dev doesn't remember consenting. Funnier, and it stops the video giving bad advice.

The punchline is a callback that changes speakers

The AI said “I have a guy” back in panel 7. Now Dev 1 says it — to a human who has no idea what he means. He's become the smug one, and he's doing to Dev 4 exactly what was done to him. The line is deliberately unhelpful, so Dev 4 ends up more confused than Dev 1 started.

The signature fires once, at the top. An earlier draft also had Dev 4 nodding the next morning before this panel — which meant he met Dev 1 twice, and asked how it was done after already nodding. Cut. The recurring line always opens; only Ep 01 repeats it at the close, because that repetition is its punchline.

What the viewer leaves with

A subagent is a scoped delegate with its own context that reports back — and it exists because of context, not politeness. The teach sits entirely inside the joke.

08

What each episode is testing

Two independent variables, one per episode. That's the argument for making three rather than one.

EpisodeHolds constantQuestion it answers
01 loop—Does the bracket structure work at all? Is it funny and educational, or neither?
02 subagentTemplateDoes a term swap need structural change? If not, the template is the product and each episode is ~6 new panels.
03 context windowCastDoes recognition survive a structure change? If yes, the characters are the brand — so shape can vary to dodge monotony without losing identity.

Ep 01 runs 20 panels: seven are fixed furniture and two more reuse earlier framing, so nine need no new art. Ep 02 lands at 17. After the first episode, each one is six to eight panels of genuinely new art — the honest answer to whether 2–3×/week survives ninety days.

09

Still to decide

  • Ep 03's ending. Still on Ep 01's restart, which §02 rules out. Inversion is the candidate; the physical-desk reframe is drafted but not adopted.
  • Bubbles or captions — pick one now. It changes every generation from here, and the existing panels assume bubbles.
  • Read panels 9–13 aloud and time them. That stretch is the comic engine and only works at speed. If the VO lingers, the episode dies.
  • Voice. One narrator for all parts, or two — one for devs, one for the AI? Two is clearer and doubles the TTS risk.
  • Does the AI get a visual signature? A consistent screen tint or caption colour, so it reads as the AI while still having no face.
  • Thumbnail. Almost certainly panel 2 — the polite blank face, plus the term. Untested.
  • Art register. These panels are the neat comic look. The crude xkcd / Sam O'Nella register is a different generation pass, and probably faster drawn by hand than prompted.
