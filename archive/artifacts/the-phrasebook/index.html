The Phrasebook

We're not starting a YouTube channel. We're writing the reference nobody has written yet — and using YouTube to make people find it.

Draft for review19 Aug 2026Open items — see 10

01 — THE SPINE

The thing that ties every video together is a book, not a format

There is no plain-English reference for the words engineers are hearing right now. Skill. MCP server. Agent. Context. Eval. Compaction. The information exists, but every version of it has a flex attached — you search for what an MCP server is and get a thread from someone announcing they built five agents last weekend.

So we write the missing version. A permanent, growing phrasebook, hosted somewhere neutral. Each entry says what the word means, why anyone should care, and one real example you could copy today.

Every video is one entry, read out loud. YouTube is the shop window. The book is the product.

Why a phrasebook and not a textbook

A phrasebook is what you carry when you've landed somewhere and don't yet speak the language. Nobody is embarrassed to carry one. The shame in not knowing something was never about looking it up — it's about having to ask a person. A reference removes the person, and the hierarchy with them.

Right now every engineer is a tourist in this, from the new grad to the staff engineer with fifteen years behind them. That's why the audience doesn't need narrowing by seniority, employment status, or age. The situation is genuinely shared.

How to say it in the room

We're writing the plain-English phrasebook for the words engineers are hearing right now. YouTube is how people find it. Every video is one entry. The channel can change format whenever we like, because the thing holding it together isn't a format — it's the book.

02 — THE FILTER

One question decides whether an idea belongs

Does it produce or clarify an entry?

That's the whole test. The point of having a spine isn't to describe what we make — it's to settle arguments about what we don't, without anyone having to win on taste.

03 — THE OBVIOUS OBJECTION

Why not just film a real person teaching?

Because a teacher teaches a syllabus that already exists. This one doesn't. The field is moving faster than anyone can write a textbook, which is precisely why the reference is missing and worth owning. A lecture delivers settled knowledge; we're documenting unsettled knowledge as it settles.

And a credentialed expert on camera rebuilds the exact hierarchy that stops a senior engineer asking in the first place. A page doesn't have a status problem. A person does.

To be clear, this doesn't rule out a human presenter — it rules out the lecture. Any delivery works, as long as what comes out the other end is an entry.

04 — THE FORMATS, DERIVED

Four ways of reading a page out loud

None of these are chosen because they're interesting. Each one is a different reading of the same object, which is why they hold together — and why a fifth can be added later, or a presenter layered on top, without any of this needing to change.

Two Voicesship first

One voice asks for the entry, the other reads it. The asker is the viewer's proxy — impatient and sceptical, never slow. Cheapest thing we can make, and it teaches us the channel's voice fastest.

The Laddership first

The same entry at three depths — to a ten-year-old, a new grad, a staff engineer. This mirrors how a real reference is built: short definition, then detail, then caveats. Highest depth signal we have, and it maps one-to-one onto the word list.

Question Compilationnext

Real, anonymised questions from engineers who feel unable to ask. This is the book's index, written by the audience — it tells us which entries to write next, in their words rather than ours.

Wrong Answer Firsthold

State an entry the way most people currently have it wrong, then correct it. Corrections are a normal part of maintaining any reference. Supply is finite, so spend it once there's an audience.

Deliberately not here

No presenter — illustrated, synthetic, or human. A presenter is an execution choice that doesn't derive from the spine, so it can be layered on later once the book is proving itself. Deciding it now would mean debating a face instead of the argument.

05 — LENGTH

Shorts and long form are different units of the same object

The argument doesn't come from attention spans. It comes from the book.

A short is one entry. A long is a cluster of related entries. Which means a short is never a cut-down of a long video — they're different sizes of the same thing, made for different jobs. Shorts do discovery; longs do depth. Playlists are chapters, ordered by which word you need before you can understand the next one.

This also settles a question that would otherwise be a hunch: we don't chop long videos into shorts, because an entry isn't a fragment of a chapter. It's a whole unit on its own.

Not yet decided

The ratio between the two. That needs the platform research in 10 before we commit — the reasoning above tells us what each format is for, not how many of each to make.

06 — BEYOND YOUTUBE

The same spine works on every other surface

  • Newsletter — this week's entries. The line already writes itself: even if you never buy our product, here are five things you can learn about AI today.
  • LinkedIn — one entry, text-first, no video needed.
  • Feature and launch videos — coaching is how an organisation writes its own entries. Same act, at org scale. The channel and the product become one story without the channel ever mentioning the product.
  • Anything we haven't thought of yet — same filter, section 02.

Worth noting for the room: this framing also sits with the existing position that coaching serves the whole distribution rather than extracting from top performers. A reference is by definition for everyone who needs to look something up.

07 — WHAT HAPPENS FIRST

Sequence

Write the word list. Fifty terms. Roughly a day's work, and everything else depends on it.

Put it in dependency order — which word has to be understood before the next one makes sense. That order becomes both the posting order and the playlist structure.

Write three entries properly. Pages, not scripts. If an entry isn't worth reading on its own, it doesn't get filmed.

Film those three — one per format, so the room can compare formats on identical content rather than in the abstract.

Name the book. It must sound like a reference and must not sound like Tetriz. It's a thing we give away, not a thing we sell.

08 — THE RISK WE'RE ACCEPTING

Two things that could sink this

The book has to be good

Good enough that someone bookmarks it and sends it to a colleague. If the videos are polished and the book is thin, we've made adverts for nothing. Mitigation: entries are always written before videos, and an entry that can't stand alone doesn't get filmed.

The payoff is slow by design

This compounds rather than spikes. Every week the book gets thicker whether or not anything performed — which is the reason it's defensible without promising subscriber numbers, but it also means it shouldn't be judged on one video's performance in month one. We should agree the review window before we start.

09 — THE COMMERCIAL ANSWER

How this connects to the product, when we want it to

Someone will ask how a channel that never mentions Tetriz is supposed to sell anything. The answer is one rule and four bridges.

The rule

The book never mentions the product. The product may cite the book.

That asymmetry is what keeps the channel credible while still letting it work commercially — and it settles future arguments cheaply, because anyone proposing a product mention inside an entry is proposing to break the one rule.

Authorshipfrom day one

The book carries a byline: maintained by the engineers at Tetriz. No claims, no CTA. Credibility transfers when someone asks who wrote this — a pull, not a push. This is the only bridge we need for the first three months.

Sales collateralanytime

The newsletter line, pointed outbound: even if you never buy our product, here are five things you can learn about AI today. Different audience, no risk to the channel, and it makes the work earn its keep immediately.

Entries only we can writemonth 3+

Tetriz reads every engineering session across an org, so some entries can carry real aggregate numbers nobody else has. How long does an agent loop actually run? What does a stuck session look like? Not a pitch — a better entry, which happens to be impossible without the product. The product becomes the research instrument behind the reference, the way a lab publishes findings.

The question the book can't answermonth 6+

Entries climb in abstraction over time. Eventually we reach how do I know if my org's AI adoption is actually working? — which has no individual answer, because it genuinely needs org-level data. The entry says so honestly. That's the handoff, and it needs no CTA, because the reader arrives at the seam themselves. Individual to org is where the bridge lives.

The precedent

DORA. A set of metrics defined in a freely-given research report, which then organised an entire product category around itself. Whoever names the problem shapes the market for the solution. That's the ambitious version of this — if the phrasebook becomes canonical, we get to name things, which is worth considerably more than a subscriber count and is a better thing to promise than growth figures.

What breaks it

Product mentions inside entries. Gating an entry behind an email. Timing entries to feature launches. And the real one — bending the word list to match the roadmap. The moment the list follows the product instead of the audience, the book is marketing, and this audience can smell that from a distance.

10 — STILL OPEN

What this needs before it's final

  • Shorts-to-long ratio. Needs a survey of five to eight adjacent channels — their actual split, and which side drives their growth — plus current reporting on what YouTube is pushing. Deliberately not decided on a hunch.
  • Reference videos, one per format, for pace and edit style. Two Voices needs one most urgently; it's the hardest of the five to picture from a written description.
  • Who writes rung three of The Ladder. The staff-engineer rung can't be written from the outside. Needs a named engineer as a standing input, or the format loses its whole advantage.
  • Where the book lives — a Tetriz domain or a neutral one. Affects how unbranded the channel can credibly claim to be.
