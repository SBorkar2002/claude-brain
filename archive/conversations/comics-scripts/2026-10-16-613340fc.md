**Mostly yes.** It teaches the core of prompt engineering correctly. But panel 19 overclaims, and one important half of the idea is missing.

**What it gets right** (checked against the workshop's Pillar 1):
- **Specific beats loud.** The crossed-out VERY, ALL and SUPER map straight onto "intensifiers are wishes, named cases are instructions." This is the strongest moment in the script.
- **Vague prompts make the AI guess, and guesses spread.** The 14 changed files and the deleted retry show what a fuzzy prompt costs. That's the workshop's scope-creep anti-pattern, and it's accurate: current models do overreach on "consider ALL edge cases."
- **The prompt contract, all four parts.** Box 1 ("expired token gives a 401, not a crash") also sneaks in "currently X, should be Y", which the workshop says a bug report needs.
- **Failure is the clause people skip, and asking costs seconds while guessing costs hours.** Panels 16 to 22 carry this well.
- **The 22 vs 21 word count** lands "it's about which details, not how many words."

**Where it falls short:**

1. **Panel 19 shrinks the term into a template.** "Not more words. The four things I can't guess" tells viewers prompt engineering *is* four boxes. The workshop says the opposite: "The discipline is being explicit. The acronym is scaffolding." A viewer who takes away "fill in the four boxes" has learned a form, not the skill. This is the one change I'd insist on. Something like: *"That's prompt engineering. Not louder. Clearer."* The boxes stay on screen as the example, but the line names the principle.

2. **It only teaches "say more."** The workshop's other half is "Too vague and it guesses. Too specific and it follows you off a cliff": be exact about what done looks like, and leave the route open. As written, the takeaway could be "the more rules the better," which is how people end up with bloated prompts. It doesn't need a new scene, just one beat. For example, in panel 15 AJ starts a fourth rule like `use a for loop`, and the AI says "That one's mine." He deletes it. That fits AJ's character too.

3. **"You never left me a box for it" (panel 24) slightly overstates.** Models do sometimes ask questions without being told to. It's funny and close enough to true that I'd keep it. The episode's point is that asking becomes *reliable* once you say so, and the line reads that way in context.

**What I'd leave out on purpose:**
- **The workshop's "~5% of what the model reads is your prompt" idea.** It's more about context and harness, and it's the territory of your context window episode. One term per episode.
- **Output schemas.** They're too technical for this audience.
